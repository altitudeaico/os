# Olatoye Family Digital Governance Framework

**First Cut | Version 0.1 | September 2026**  
**Status:** Discussion Draft

> Freedom within boundaries.  
> The Firm carries the risks the child is not yet equipped to carry.  
> Capability earns autonomy.

## 1. Purpose

The Olatoye family intends to be digitally forward. The girls should learn to use technology confidently and productively, including AI, coding, research, content creation, design, communication, presenting and building online.

The purpose of this framework is not to minimise technology. It is to make deliberate decisions about what digital capabilities are available, under what conditions, and who carries the associated risk.

## 2. Where this sits

### The Firm
The enduring family institution. It owns the governance, protection, security, assets, reputation and risks that should not yet sit with a child.

### Olatoye Academy
Develops the girls' knowledge, judgement, confidence and capability so that they can participate increasingly in the digital world and, over time, in The Firm.

### Family OS
The operating infrastructure that turns family decisions into systems, permissions, workflows and controls.

### Guardian
The safeguarding and policy layer that enforces appropriate boundaries across devices, content, accounts and online activity.

## 3. Core principles

**Freedom within boundaries.** We want meaningful digital freedom, but inside deliberately engineered boundaries rather than unrestricted access.

**The Firm carries immature risk.** The Firm carries risks the child is not yet equipped to carry, including account security, public exposure, external contact, commercial relationships and reputation.

**Capability earns autonomy.** Autonomy expands as judgement, responsibility and digital capability are demonstrated. Age matters, but age alone does not determine access.

**Private by default.** Personal information, identity, location and family information should begin from a private position and be exposed deliberately rather than accidentally.

**Creation is not the same as consumption.** A child can be given extensive freedom to create without automatically receiving unrestricted feeds, browsing or social interaction.

**Protection is not surveillance.** We collect and observe only what is necessary to protect. The aim is to build judgement and trust, not to watch everything.

**Governance sits above technology.** Technology and AI operate within family rules. Available features do not determine what the family permits.

**Defence in depth.** Important risks should not depend on one control or on a child remembering a rule. Policy, identity, device controls, filtering, approvals and education should reinforce one another.

## 4. The capability model

Instead of treating a device or platform as simply 'allowed' or 'not allowed', access should be separated into capabilities. Each capability can have its own level of freedom.

| Capability | What it includes | Starting posture |
|---|---|---|
| **CREATE** | Write, film, design, code, edit, make music, build, use creative AI. | High freedom within age-appropriate tools. |
| **PUBLISH** | Upload, post, share publicly, choose captions/thumbnails, manage a creator presence. | Permitted through governed accounts and appropriate approval. |
| **CONSUME** | Browse, search, watch, read and scroll. | Curated or filtered access appropriate to the child and context. |
| **INTERACT** | Comment, message, follow, friend, join chats, livestream and communicate with others. | Most tightly controlled; expands deliberately with judgement. |

## 5. Governance domains

**Devices & internet** — Which devices can be used, when, in which modes, and what network/content controls apply.

**Privacy & personal data** — What information about the girls and family may be collected, stored, uploaded or shared.

**Identity & public exposure** — Use of names, faces, locations, routines, family relationships, photographs and other identifying information.

**Social media & creator presence** — Account ownership, publishing, feeds, followers, comments, DMs, livestreaming and creator activity.

**Accounts & cyber security** — Passwords, MFA, recovery, software updates, downloads, app installation, phishing and compromised accounts.

**Communication** — Who may contact the girls, through which channels, and what happens when an unknown person attempts contact.

**AI & external services** — Which AI services may be used, what may be uploaded, what personal information may be shared, and when adult oversight is required.

**Monitoring & safeguarding** — What parents/Guardian may observe, what triggers intervention, what is logged and what remains private.

## 6. Creator presence

The family should distinguish between a child learning to be a digital creator and a child independently operating a social-media account.

A Creator Presence can allow the girls to learn the complete creator workflow while The Firm retains responsibility for the external perimeter.

- The child may progressively create, script, film, present, edit, design thumbnails, draft captions, review analytics and participate in publishing.
- The Firm retains control of account ownership, credentials, recovery, privacy/security settings, external messages, commercial relationships and any other risk not yet appropriate for the child.
- Publishing can be available without automatically enabling unrestricted direct messages, stranger contact, livestreaming or an unrestricted algorithmic feed.
- Under a platform's minimum age, the family does not falsify age to create an unsupervised child account. Parent-owned or supported supervised arrangements should be used instead.

## 7. Public exposure

Identity risk is cumulative. A first name in isolation is different from a first name combined with surname, precise location, routine, club, home exterior and live whereabouts. The framework should therefore govern combinations of information, not rely on a blanket rule such as 'never use names'.

Initial publication guardrails for discussion:

- No live location or real-time indication that the family is away from home.
- No home address, personal phone number, personal email address or other direct contact information.
- Avoid exposing predictable routines, regular venues or unnecessary location detail.
- No child-controlled direct messages with unknown people.
- Livestreaming requires specific adult permission and supervision.
- Materially revealing content should require adult approval before publication.

## 8. Access levels

The detailed controls do not need to be identical for both girls. The framework should support progressive access levels rather than a single family-wide setting.

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1. Guided** | Adult-led access to approved tools and content. |
| **2. Governed** | Independent use inside tightly defined technical boundaries. |
| **3. Trusted** | Broader choice and selected external interaction with oversight. |
| **4. Independent** | The child carries substantially more responsibility for their own digital decisions. |

## 9. What Version 1 needs to decide

This first cut deliberately establishes the philosophy before prescribing every rule. Bolaji and Funmi should use Version 1 to make the first set of explicit family decisions.

- Which capabilities Elsie and Emma currently have across Create, Publish, Consume and Interact.
- Which activities always require adult approval and which can be performed independently.
- The family's initial rules for names, faces, location, routines and other public information.
- How creator accounts are owned and operated by The Firm.
- What kind of feeds, browsing and search are permitted at each stage.
- Rules for DMs, comments, followers, gaming/social chat and unknown contacts.
- Baseline device and account security requirements.
- Which AI tools are permitted and what information may be shared with them.
- What Guardian should technically enforce and what should remain a behavioural/educational rule.
- What evidence of judgement or responsibility allows autonomy to expand, and what events temporarily reduce it.

## 10. Intended outcome

We do not protect our children from the digital world. We prepare them to thrive within it, while protecting them from risks they are not yet equipped to carry.

Success is not that the girls remain permanently dependent on controls. Success is that they become unusually capable digital citizens who understand privacy, security, reputation, creation, communication and risk, and who progressively require fewer controls because their judgement has grown.
